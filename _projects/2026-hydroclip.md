          <p class="hc-kicker">Project Overview</p>
          <h2>Redesigning a prototype for production</h2>

          <p>HydroClip is a backpack mounted water bottle holder built around a custom bottle holder, buckle insert, buckle receiver, mounting plate, and strap clamps. My work focused on taking the prototype beyond a functional concept and reworking the product around how its parts could actually be manufactured, assembled, and scaled.</p>

          <h3>Design for manufacturing</h3>
          <p>I selected polypropylene for the primary custom components because the geometry was well suited to injection molding and the material offered a practical balance of cost, durability, and impact resistance. I then redesigned several features to reduce tooling complexity. The strap interface was changed to use simple molded grooves with silicone inlays instead of relying on a complex grip texture molded directly into the plastic.</p>

          <p>I also consolidated the mounting plate and buckle receiver into a single component, eliminating a separate mold and reducing the number of parts that would need to be assembled. For the release clip, I separated the design by manufacturing process: the T shaped body remained injection molded while the buckle arms were redesigned as a laser cut part. This kept the molded geometry simpler while using laser cutting for the thin two dimensional spring feature.</p>

          <h3>Design for assembly</h3>
          <p>The prototype exposed several opportunities for assembly error, especially when adhesive bonded parts had to be aligned by hand. I added poka yoke features to make correct placement more obvious and repeatable. An alignment ring prevents the bottle holder from being installed in the wrong orientation, a circular guide locates the internal spring during assembly, and six locating tabs position the receiver on the mounting plate.</p>

          <h3>Sustainability and next steps</h3>
          <p>I also evaluated the product beyond manufacturing cost. A carbon footprint estimate identified distribution and assembly as the largest contributors, so I considered alternative material and process strategies including recycled PET, PLA, thermoforming, and lighter recyclable packaging. My final recommendation was to extend development before production so material testing, process trials, CAD refinement, and user testing could be completed before committing to full scale manufacturing.</p>
