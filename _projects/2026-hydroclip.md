        <p class="hc-kicker">Project Overview</p>
        <h2>Redesigning a functional prototype for production</h2>

        <p>HydroClip is a backpack mounted water bottle holder built around a custom bottle holder, buckle insert, buckle receiver, mounting plate, and strap clamps. The project began as a working prototype and was then redesigned around how the product could be manufactured, assembled, and scaled more effectively.</p>

        <h3>Design for manufacturing</h3>
        <p>I selected polypropylene for the primary custom components because it offered a practical balance of cost, durability, impact resistance, and compatibility with injection molding. I then simplified several features that would otherwise increase tooling complexity. The strap interface was redesigned with molded grooves for silicone inlays, providing grip without requiring an intricate texture to be built directly into the mold.</p>

        <p>The mounting plate and buckle receiver were consolidated into a single component to eliminate a separate mold and reduce assembly work. I also separated the release clip by manufacturing process: the T shaped body remained injection molded while the thin buckle arms were redesigned as a laser cut component. This kept the molded geometry simpler while preserving the precision and flexibility needed in the spring feature.</p>

        <h3>Design for assembly</h3>
        <p>The prototype also exposed several opportunities for assembly error, especially where bonded parts had to be aligned manually. I added poka yoke features to make the correct orientation and placement more obvious. An alignment ring prevents the bottle holder from being installed upside down, a circular feature locates the internal spring, and six locating tabs position the receiver on the mounting plate.</p>

        <h3>Sustainability and development</h3>
        <p>I evaluated the product beyond manufacturability by estimating its carbon footprint and identifying distribution and assembly as the largest contributors. I considered alternative materials and processes including recycled PET, PLA, thermoforming, and lighter recyclable packaging. My final recommendation was to extend development before production so material testing, process trials, CAD refinement, and user testing could be completed before committing to full scale manufacturing.</p>
