        <div class="hc-copy-intro">
          <p class="hc-kicker">Project Overview</p>
          <h2>Redesigning a working concept for production</h2>
          <p class="hc-lede">HydroClip is a backpack mounted water bottle holder built around a custom bottle holder, buckle insert, buckle receiver, mounting plate, and strap clamps. The project began as a functional prototype and was then redesigned around how the product could be manufactured, assembled, and scaled more effectively.</p>
        </div>

        <div class="hc-copy-grid">
          <section>
            <h3>Design for manufacturing</h3>
            <p>I selected polypropylene for the primary custom components because it offered a practical balance of cost, durability, impact resistance, and compatibility with injection molding. The strap interface was redesigned with molded grooves for silicone inlays, providing grip without requiring a complex molded surface texture.</p>

            <p>The mounting plate and buckle receiver were consolidated into a single component to eliminate a separate mold and reduce assembly work. I also split the release clip by manufacturing process: the T shaped body remained injection molded while the thin buckle arms were redesigned as a laser cut component. This simplified the molded geometry while preserving the flexibility required by the spring feature.</p>
          </section>

          <section>
            <h3>Design for assembly</h3>
            <p>The prototype exposed several opportunities for assembly error, especially where bonded parts had to be aligned manually. I added alignment and locating features to make orientation and placement more repeatable. An alignment ring prevents the bottle holder from being installed upside down, a circular feature locates the internal spring, and six locating tabs position the receiver on the mounting plate.</p>

            <h3>Sustainability and development</h3>
            <p>The sustainability model estimated a total footprint of 12.28 kg CO₂e, with distribution and assembly as the largest contributors. I also evaluated alternative materials and processes including recycled PET, PLA, thermoforming, and lighter recyclable packaging before recommending additional material testing, process trials, CAD refinement, and user testing prior to full scale manufacturing.</p>
          </section>
